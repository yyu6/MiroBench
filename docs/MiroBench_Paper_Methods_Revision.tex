\subsection{Benchmark Metrics}
\label{sec:scores}

MiroBench evaluates each discussion using 12 thread-level metrics organized
into five families: Uniformity, Expression, Tone, Interaction, and Form.

\textbf{Uniformity.}
This family measures how strongly comments in the same thread repeat similar
wording or meaning.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{self\_bleu\_4} averages symmetric pairwise BLEU-4 across
    comments. Higher values indicate more repeated phrasing and lower lexical
    diversity \citep{zhu2018texygen}.

    \item \texttt{self\_bertscore\_mean\_f1} averages pairwise BERTScore F1
    across comments. Higher values indicate stronger contextual similarity
    even when the wording differs \citep{zhang2019bertscore,zhang2024improving}.

    \item \texttt{semantic\_mean\_cosine} embeds comments with
    \texttt{sentence-transformers/all-mpnet-base-v2} and averages pairwise
    cosine similarity. Higher values indicate that comments discuss a narrower
    range of meanings \citep{reimers2019sentence,song2020mpnet}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Expression.}
This family measures personal storytelling and the range of emotions expressed
within a thread.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{mean\_story\_probability} applies StorySeeker to each
    eligible comment and averages its probability of containing a personal
    story. Higher values indicate more first-person narratives
    \citep{antoniak2024people}.

    \item \texttt{emotion\_entropy} assigns each eligible comment its dominant
    emotion using \texttt{SamLowe/roberta-base-go\_emotions} and computes
    Shannon entropy over the label distribution. Higher values indicate a
    wider range of emotions.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Tone.}
This family measures the interpersonal tone of comments.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{polite\_rate}, \texttt{neutral\_rate}, and
    \texttt{impolite\_rate} are the proportions of eligible comments assigned
    to the corresponding labels by \texttt{Intel/polite-guard}. The classifier
    may also assign \texttt{somewhat\_polite}; therefore, the three reported
    rates do not have to sum to one.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Interaction.}
This family measures reply depth, disagreement, and the shape of the reply
network.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{avg\_depth} is the mean comment depth in the reply tree,
    where top-level comments have depth 1. Higher values indicate deeper reply
    chains.

    \item \texttt{hard\_disagree\_rate} is the proportion of valid
    parent--reply pairs classified as disagreement. It measures opposing stance
    rather than surface politeness.

    \item \texttt{structural\_virality} treats parent--reply links between
    comments as an undirected graph and averages shortest-path distance over
    reachable unordered comment pairs. Higher values indicate longer and more
    distributed reply paths \citep{goel2016structural}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Form.}
This family measures variation in comment length within a thread.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{length\_cv} is the coefficient of variation of comment
    length. Higher values indicate more variation between short and long
    comments relative to the thread's average comment length.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol and Metric Formulas}
\label{sec:eval_process}

Let $t=(c_1,\ldots,c_{n_t})$ be a thread with $n_t$ eligible comments. Let
$\mathcal{P}(t)=\{(i,j):1\leq i<j\leq n_t\}$ be its unordered comment pairs,
and let $M_t=|\mathcal{P}(t)|$. For comment $c_i$, let $e_i$ be its sentence
embedding, $\ell_i$ its token length, $d_i$ its reply depth, and $s_i$ its
StorySeeker probability. Let $y_i$ be its dominant emotion and let $p_{t,e}$
be the proportion of comments assigned emotion $e$. Let $z_{i,k}$ indicate
whether comment $i$ receives tone label $k$. Finally, let $\mathcal{E}(t)$ be
the set of valid parent--reply pairs and let $a_{ij}$ indicate whether pair
$(i,j)$ is classified as disagreement.

Here, $G_t$ is the undirected comment reply graph and $\mathcal{Q}(t)$ is the
set of reachable unordered comment pairs in that graph. The root post is not a
node in the current structural-virality implementation. Metrics have different
eligibility rules. Pairwise Uniformity metrics require at least two eligible
comments, while \texttt{hard\_disagree\_rate} requires at least one valid
parent--reply pair. We report metric coverage with every comparison.

For metric $f$, the real and generated thread-score distributions for domain
$d$, baseline $b$, and model $m$ are
\[
R_f^{(d)}=\{f(t):t\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{real}}^{(d)}\},\qquad
G_f^{(d,b,m)}=\{f(t):t\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{gen}}^{(d,b,m)}\}.
\]
We compare these distributions using two-sided Mann--Whitney U and
Kolmogorov--Smirnov tests. We also report the first Wasserstein distance and
absolute Cliff's delta:
\[
\begin{aligned}
W_1(R_f,G_f)
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|F_{R_f}(x)-F_{G_f}(x)|\,dx,\\
|\delta_{\mathrm{Cliff}}(R_f,G_f)|
&=\left|\frac{1}{|R_f||G_f|}
\sum_{r\in R_f}\sum_{g\in G_f}\operatorname{sgn}(g-r)\right|.
\end{aligned}
\]

We use $\alpha=0.05$. A value of $p\geq0.05$ means that the test does not
detect a difference at this threshold; it does not prove that the two
distributions are equal. Lower Wasserstein distance and lower absolute Cliff's
delta indicate a smaller generated-vs.-real gap for every metric. Because
Wasserstein distance uses each metric's original scale, it is compared only
within the same metric. We report raw per-metric $p$-values and apply a declared
multiple-testing correction when making aggregate significance claims.

\subsection{Sanity Check: Repeated Real-vs.-Real Validation}
\label{sec:real_real_sanity}

We use repeated real-vs.-real comparisons to calibrate the evaluation. For
each domain, we draw two independent bootstrap samples of 150 threads from the
fixed 150-thread real reference. Sampling is with replacement, so a source
thread may occur more than once within a sample or in both samples. We repeat
this process 200 times for each of the 12 metrics and apply the same tests and
distance measures used in the main evaluation.

Across the 28,800 domain-metric comparisons, Mann--Whitney U $p$-values are at
least 0.05 in 95.10\% of comparisons, and Kolmogorov--Smirnov $p$-values are at
least 0.05 in 96.97\%. Both tests pass in 94.34\% of comparisons. Across the
12 domains, the Mann--Whitney pass rate ranges from 93.17\% to 96.17\%, and the
Kolmogorov--Smirnov pass rate ranges from 95.75\% to 98.42\%. These results
show the expected false-positive behavior under the bootstrap null. They do
not by themselves show that generated discussions match real Reddit data.

We use the 95th percentile of each real-vs.-real Wasserstein and absolute
Cliff's-delta distribution as an empirical reference limit for the same metric
and domain. A generated-vs.-real value below this limit lies within the upper
95\% of the observed real-vs.-real discrepancies. This comparison is a
calibration aid, not a formal equivalence test.
